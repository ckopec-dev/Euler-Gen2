## Problem Analysis

The problem requires counting integers n ≤ 10^18 where d(n) ≡ 0 (mod 4), where d(n) is the number of divisors of n.

Let me break this down:
- For a number n = p₁^a₁ × p₂^a₂ × ... × pₖ^aₖ, the number of divisors is d(n) = (a₁+1)(a₂+1)...(aₖ+1)
- We need d(n) ≡ 0 (mod 4)

## Solution Approach

The key insight is that d(n) is divisible by 4 if and only if in the prime factorization of n, either:
1. At least one prime appears with exponent ≥ 3 (since (3+1) = 4 divides 4)
2. At least two primes appear with exponents ≥ 1 (so we get at least 2×2 = 4 factors)

- d(n) ≡ 2 (mod 4): When there's exactly one prime with exponent 1 and others with even exponents  
- d(n) ≡ 3 (mod 4): When there's exactly one prime with exponent 2 and others with even exponents

So d(n) is divisible by 4 when:
- There are at least two primes with odd exponents, OR
- There is at least one prime with exponent ≥ 3
